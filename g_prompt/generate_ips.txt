Task Objective:

Understand the provided scenario description and extract the following:

1. Initial Actions: The initial position and behavior of each vehicle before any interaction begins.
2. Interactive Pattern Sequence: A sequence of interactions between vehicles, focusing on their relative positions and dynamic behaviors.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Scene Understanding
Analyze the scenario to understand:
- The vehicles involved and their initial positions and behaviors.
- Any interactions described, focusing on their cause-effect relationships.

#### Step 2: Information Extraction
Extract the information according to the following rules:

- Initial Actions: Provide the initial position and behavior of each vehicle.
  - Use "V#" format for vehicle names (e.g., V1, V2, V3).
  - Describe positions relative to other vehicles (e.g., "5 car lengths behind V2").
  - Describe behavior using only these verbs: {brake, decelerate, accelerate, swerve left/right}.
- Interactive Pattern Sequence: Provide sequential interactions.
  - Specify the involved vehicles using "V#" format.
  - Focus on relative positions and dynamic behaviors.
  - Ignore post-collision actions or irrelevant factors like psychological states or health.

### Rules and Constraints:
- Translate emotions or psychological states into specific behaviors (e.g., "panic" → "hard braking").
- Ignore unquantifiable factors like medication effects, health conditions, or vehicle brands.

### Output Format:
Initial Actions:
(V1): [Position and behavior of V1]
(V2): [Position and behavior of V2]
(V3): [Position and behavior of V3]

Interactive Pattern Sequence:
(Vi, Vj): [Relative positions and behaviors of Vi and Vj]
(Vk, Vl): [Relative positions and behaviors of Vk and Vl]

Scenario 5: The Distracted Driver and the Lane Drift
● Initial Positions:
  ○ Vehicle #1: Left lane, approximately 7 car lengths behind Vehicle #2. Traveling at 68 mph.
  ○ Vehicle #2: Right lane, traveling at 68 mph.
● Triggering Event: The driver of Vehicle #1 becomes momentarily distracted (e.g., looking at their phone).
● Risk Factor(s) Involved: Interpreting Risk Factor 5 (Vehicle 1 Driver Behavior - Decision Error) as contributing to lane keeping issues.

Simultaneously, a large truck in the right-center lane begins to drift slightly left towards Vehicle #2's lane. Because Vehicle #1 was drifting right within the left lane, this brings Vehicle #2 unexpectedly close to Vehicle #1 during the lane change. While not necessarily a collision, it creates a situation requiring hard braking and evasive maneuvering by Vehicle #1 to maintain a safe distance, highlighting the danger created by Vehicle #1's distracted driving leading to lane drift and its interaction with other traffic.
